Raising prices in Jobber

How to raise prices on recurring jobs in Jobber

In Jobber, prices sit on each recurring job, so each job’s price is changed by hand. The client export, the client mass update and the job import don’t touch job prices. The Recurring Jobs Report is the one place that lists every recurring job with its line items and total.

Why the bulk tools don’t help

  • The client export gives you contact details, tags, addresses and custom fields. It has no prices.
  • The client mass update (a re-import of that file) changes client settings, tags, text custom fields and details like a name or phone number. Prices sit on jobs, not clients.
  • The job import (a Beta) brings jobs in. A job whose number is already taken gets the next free number instead of replacing the old job, so you’d end up with two.

“…Exporting your client list includes information like tags, property addresses, contact information, or custom fields…”

Jobber Help: Export client information, read 5 November 2026

Get your real price list out

  1. On jobber.com, select Insights, then Reports, then Recurring jobs under Work reports.
  2. Add the columns you need: Job #, Client name, Billing type, Line items and Total ($).
  3. Click Export to CSV and choose all columns or the selected ones. Jobber emails the file to the address you log in with.

That file has one row per recurring job: the price list you actually have. The Billing type column shows whether a job is billed per visit or at a fixed price.

“The recurring jobs report lists your recurring jobs along with important details about them.”

Jobber Help: Recurring Jobs Report, read 5 November 2026

Then change the prices

  1. Work out each job’s new price and the date it starts.
  2. Tell each client before the first visit or invoice at the new price.
  3. Edit the line items on each recurring job in Jobber, and tick it off.

Don’t try the import as a shortcut

Re-importing your jobs with new prices won’t update them. Jobber’s own example: with jobs 1 to 5 already in place, importing jobs numbered 3, 4, 5 and 6 turns the 3, 4 and 5 into new jobs 7, 8 and 9.

“…the imported job will use the next highest available job number…”

Jobber Help: Import jobs, read 5 November 2026

The catch. The edit in Jobber is still manual, one recurring job at a time. Price Raise reads the Recurring Jobs Report export, works out each new price, sends the notices and gives you a “Price changes to make” list with each job number, so the clicking is all that’s left.

Price Raise loads your client list, works out each new price from your rule, shows what you gain and what you could lose, writes a personal notice for each client and tracks the replies.
